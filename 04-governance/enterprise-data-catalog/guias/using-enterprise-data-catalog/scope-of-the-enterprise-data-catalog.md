# Scope of the Enterprise Data Catalog

After defining your objective, outline the scope by identifying the relevant data sources, systems, and stakeholders involved.

The following are the steps to determine the scope of the Enterprise Data Catalog:

### Data Sources

- Identify the data assets that must be cataloged, including databases, data lakes, files, and SaaS applications.
- **Business relevance**: Prioritize the data sources that are most critical for business operations and analytics.

### Systems

- Determine the systems that you need to integrate with the Enterprise Data Catalog.
- **Metadata collection**: Understand how to automate metadata collection from various data systems to ensure the catalog is always up-to-date.

### Stakeholders

- **Identify the data owners**: They are responsible for managing the data and ensuring its quality and security.
- **Identify the data stewards**: They play a key role in curating, managing metadata, and governing data use within the catalog.
- **Identify the end users**: End users may include analysts, data scientists, and business users who regularly access the Enterprise Data Catalog for their data needs.
- Identify any other responsibility type that needs to be defined.

### How to define the scope of the Enterprise Data Catalog?

- **Conduct a data inventory**: Perform a thorough inventory of all data sources within the organization.
- **Establish governance roles**: Define who will manage, curate, and control access to the Enterprise Data Catalog.
- **Focus on high-value data**: Start with critical datasets that provide the most value for decision-making and expand as necessary.

### Best practices

- **Start small, then scale**: Begin with a pilot phase focusing on a limited number of high-value datasets, then expand the catalog as adoption grows.
- **Regularly review and update**: Continuously evaluate the data catalog’s goals and scope to adapt to the evolving needs of the organization.
