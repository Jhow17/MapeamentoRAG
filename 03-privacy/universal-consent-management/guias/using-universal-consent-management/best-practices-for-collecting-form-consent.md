# Best practices for collecting form consent

The consent of data subjects is one of the lawful bases of personal data processing under most global privacy laws. It is especially needed for using personal data for advertising and marketing purposes. The following sections provide an overview of global best practices in terms of obtaining consent from users for using their personal data for direct marketing purposes.

### Opt-in consent (default unchecked)

Most countries including New Zealand, Canada, Australia, Hong Kong, Singapore, the United Kingdom and all European Union countries require you to obtain explicit opt-in consent from individuals prior to sending them marketing communications.

This requires you to ensure the following steps:

- Show the checkbox on the website for users to select whether they want to receive marketing communications.
- Do not pre-mark checkboxes (default unchecked).
- Provide the option opt-out in every subsequent marketing communication by including language at the bottom of the marketing communication that instructs users how they can opt-out.

For example: "If you do not wish to receive further marketing emails from us, please click here."

### Double opt-in consent (default unchecked and email confirmation)

The double opt-in practice has been developed over years in Germany. You must ensure the following steps to fulfill the double opt-in requirement:

- Show the checkbox on the website for users to select whether they want to receive marketing communications.
- Do not pre-mark checkboxes (default unchecked).
- Once a user opts-in via checkbox, send them an email confirmation to confirm their subscription.
- Provide the option to opt-out in every subsequent marketing communication by including language at the bottom of the marketing communication that instructs users how they can opt-out.

For example: "If you do not wish to receive further marketing emails from us, please click here."

### Opt-out consent (default checked)

A very few countries such as the United States allow you to obtain opt-out consent from individuals prior to sending them marketing communications. You must ensure the following steps to fulfill the opt-out consent requirement:

- Inform the user that their personal data will be used for sending marketing communications at or before the point of collection of personal data via a checkbox and provide them the option to opt-out.
- You can pre-mark the checkbox and users can uncheck if they want to opt-out (default checked checkbox).
- Provide the option to opt-out in every subsequent marketing communication by including language at the bottom of the marketing communication that instructs users how they can opt-out.

For example: "If you do not wish to receive further marketing emails from us, please click here."

### Once a user opts-out

For all countries (opt-in, double opt-in and opt-out), you must ensure the following once a user has opted-out:

- Do not send them any further marketing communications and do not contact them to invite them to opt-back into marketing.
- Stop the use of any profiling data relating to that user and remove any profiling data of the user.
- Honor opt-out requests promptly. Do not unduly postpone or delay an opt-out request.

Keeping in view data minimization and storage limitation principles of the GDPR, you must ensure the following with respect to consent records on EU personal data such as personal data which is granted protection under the GDPR:

- Delete consent records of individuals who have opted out or withdrawn their consent assuming there is no other lawful basis justifying the continued retention of consent records.
- You can prescribe a data retention policy depending on the purposes of personal data processing that lets you automatically delete personal data after the completion of the intended purposes of the data processing.
- To know further about deleting consent records, see [Deleting consent records from the dashboard](/v1/docs/using-the-universal-consent-management-dashboard).

### Marketing communications to existing customers

Most privacy laws such as the **e-Privacy Directive** allow organizations to send marketing communications to individuals whose details they obtained in the context of the sale of a product or service without making them select an opt-in checkbox (default checked opt-out checkbox), provided that the following conditions are fulfilled:

- You obtained the individual’s contact details in the context of the sale of its product or service.
- The individual did not opt-out at the time of providing his/her personal data and a clear and distinct opt-out ability was provided to the individual.
- You can send marketing messages only about your own products and services and similar products and services for which the individual’s details were originally collected. Do not send them information about different products or services when that is not within the reasonable expectations of the individual.
- You must clearly and distinctly remind individuals about their ability to opt-out at every subsequent marketing communication.
- Opt-out should be simple, free-of-charge, both at the time of the collection of the personal data from individuals and in each subsequent marketing communication.

The interpretation of the term “in the context of the sale of a product or service” differs from one regulation to another. For example:

- Most EU countries allow you to send marketing communications to existing customers via opt-out checkbox only if a sale transaction has taken place with them.
  - There has to be a transaction (purchase/service agreement) in which you were or are obliged to deliver something and the customer to pay for it. Someone is not an existing customer if they have not yet purchased anything but only registered for your newsletter, completed a survey, participated in a contest/game or created a user account.
  - An actual sale must have taken place as opposed to a mere negotiation.
  - If the electronic address is provided by the customer in connection with the receipt of a gift, free advice, an offer, the download of a sales quotation, or an app, participation in a competition or the like, this does not constitute a purchase and the company requires the explicit opt-in consent of the customer before the company is permitted to send marketing communications.
- Australia allows you to send marketing communications to customers via opt-out checkbox only if there is an ongoing customer relationship with them.

Where the recipient has knowingly and directly given his/her email address and it is reasonable to believe they would expect to receive marketing from your business. "Reasonable to believe" means a provable and ongoing relationship with your business and the marketing is directly relevant to that relationship. For example, if someone has subscribed to a service, has an account or is a member, and the marketing is relevant to the relationship. It does not cover sending marketing messages after someone has just bought something from your business.
- The United Kingdom allows you to send marketing communications to customers via opt-out checkbox after a sale or even if just negotiations for a sale took place with them.

The customer does not actually have to have bought anything. It is enough if “negotiations for a sale” took place. For example, a customer logs into a company's website to browse its range of products. This is not enough to constitute negotiations. But if the customer completes an online enquiry form asking for more details about a product or range of products, this could be enough. This means that the customer should have actively expressed an interest in buying an organization's products or services - for example, by requesting a quote, or asking for more details of what it offers. There must be some sort of express communication.

### Use of purchased marketing lists

For opt-in and double opt-in countries, you should not use a purchased marketing list as a general rule. You may rely on a purchased marketing list only if all the individuals on the list specifically consented to receive marketing communications from you. Generic consent to receive marketing communications from any third-party will not be enough. They would have to agree to be receiving marketing communications specifically from you.

This may require you to undertake due diligence on whether the users' consent was obtained and users were provided with your name and contact details and they explicitly agreed to share their personal data with you for the purposes of sending marketing communications. You must cross-reference purchased marketing lists against your own "Do-Not-Contact" list of people who have previously objected to or opted-out of your marketing communications.

### Common email marketing best practices

- Ensure that the marketing message is clearly identifiable as a commercial communication. It should not, for example, attempt to present itself as a personal communication to the recipient.
- Ensure that any promotional offers such as discounts, premiums and gifts are clearly identifiable as such and that the conditions to qualify for them are easily accessible and presented clearly and unambiguously.
- Ensure that any promotional competitions or games (if permitted at all in the relevant jurisdiction) are clearly identifiable as such and that the conditions for participation are easily accessible and presented clearly and unambiguously.
- Do not conceal or disguise the identity of the sender on whose behalf the communication is made.
- Do not export personal data outside of the jurisdiction unless cross-border data protection rules of the relevant jurisdiction are taken into consideration and adequate protection is in place. Contact lists should not be sent to overseas advertising agencies without ensuring adequate protection is in place and cross-border data transfer conditions are met.
- Do not use false or misleading header information: Your “from”, “to”, “reply-to” and routing information – including the originating domain name and email address must be accurate and identify the person or business who initiated the message (**US CAN-SPAM Act**).
- Do not use deceptive subject lines (**US CAN-SPAM Act**).
- Tell recipients where you are located with a valid physical postal address. This can be your current street address, a post office box you have registered with the U.S. Postal Service, or a private mailbox you’ve registered with a commercial mail receiving agency established under Postal Service regulations (**US CAN-SPAM Act**).
- Tell recipients how to opt-out of receiving future email from you (**US CAN-SPAM Act**).Honor opt-out requests promptly and maximum within **10 business days** (**US CAN-SPAM Act**).

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is an European Union (EU) regulation on information privacy. It aims to ensure the protection of personal information through a human rights-centric approach, and imposes obligations onto organizations anywhere, so long as they target or collect data related to people in the EU.
