# Using Data Subject Rights

Modern privacy regulations such as GDPR and CPRA grant consumers broad rights to the personal data that enterprises collect from them and about them. Depending upon their locations, consumers can request access to their data, that you stop processing it, or that you delete it. Such requests are known as Data Subject Requests or Customer Data Requests. Enterprises must fulfill such requests promptly, with only a few exceptions allowed for legal reasons.

One such request from a consumer can translate into large numbers of often unknown tasks that you must perform inside your company. Without an automated system to correlate identity and personal data, the resolution of data subject requests can translate into manual investigations to:

- Verify the identity of the person making the subject request.
- Discover which systems and which objects within those systems hold Data subject' data. A typical enterprise may have hundreds or thousands of such internal and external systems.
- Discover current owners of those systems and objects. In a typical enterprise, the ownership changes regularly.
- Engage owners of systems and objects over email or other methods and share the details of the subject.
- Work with each system and object owner to comply with the request. The actions required vary depending upon the request type and the legal reasons for data retention.
- Combine the products of all parts of the investigation into one report for approval by the stakeholders and the legal team.
- Securely share the report with the data subject.
- Keep an audit trail of all the steps taken to comply with the request, to prove compliance in case of legal issues.

Doing all the above tasks manually for each data subject request can be costly, inefficient, and most importantly, prone to human errors that result in lapses of compliance.

The Securiti Data Subject Rights module is an automated system that drastically simplifies the process of fully complying with data subject rights requests.

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is an European Union (EU) regulation on information privacy. It aims to ensure the protection of personal information through a human rights-centric approach, and imposes obligations onto organizations anywhere, so long as they target or collect data related to people in the EU.

The California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA) amended the CCPA by adding additional consumer privacy rights and obligations for businesses. It also created the California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA), tasked with enforcing the law and educating the public on their rights and obligations under the law.
