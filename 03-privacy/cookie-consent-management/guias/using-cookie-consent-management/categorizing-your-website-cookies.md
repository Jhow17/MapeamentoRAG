# Categorizing your website cookies

**Scenario**

As a Data Compliance Officer, you must report the types of cookies your website uses. For instance, when preparing a compliance report, manually reviewing cookies across websites can be inefficient and may lead to misclassification. With Securiti’s built-in cookie scanner, you can automatically group cookies by purpose or reclassify them as needed. This helps you complete reviews faster and reduce the risk of noncompliance.

After you scan your websites to see what cookies they drop, you inspect the Cookie categories that Securiti chose for the different cookies, and adjust them if necessary.

Securiti automatically classifies cookies and trackers into **eight** out-of-the-box (OOTB) categories to help streamline consent management and regulatory compliance. These OOTB cookie categories include:

- Essential
- Advertising
- Analytics & Customizations
- Performance & Functionality
- Social Networking
- Unclassified (Includes cookies and trackers that Securiti cannot match to any known record in its extensive cookie knowledge base.)
- Biometrics
- Precise Geolocation

Each cookie category reflects a common purpose for which cookies and tracking technologies are used across domains, helping you manage consent with greater clarity and control.

For more information on how Securiti cookie categorization engine works, see [Understanding how Securiti categorizes cookies and trackers](/v1/docs/understanding-how-securiti-categorizes-cookies-and-trackers).

> [!WARNING]
> **Important**
> 
> Securiti has upgraded its cookie categorization process for a smoother experience on its management portal. Dynamic cookies, identified by variable numbers and fixed prefixes or suffixes, often cause confusion in the **Unclassified** cookie category. In response, our Knowledge Base Engineering (KBE) team actively refines the data for new domain scans, removing duplicate dynamic cookies to improve the accuracy of default cookie categorization.
> 
> Following the intervention of Securiti’s KB team, if a rescan occurs on the same domain, the dynamic cookies are consolidated to drastically reduce the redundancy. This ongoing process ensures a streamlined experience, particularly when scanning new domains. Despite this effort, occasional appearances of dynamic cookies in the **Unclassified** cookie category may occur post-scan. Contact the **Customer Success Manager** or **Account Representative** if you face such a scenario.

The configuration of cookies and categorizations becomes a cookie policy. This procedure describes the initial process, see [Managing cookie policies](/v1/docs/managing-cookie-policies) for more information about the ongoing process.

## Classification of Securiti's `privaci cookies`

There are some cookies that belong to Securiti that we use to manage the cookie consent banners and the consent preferences of your data subjects and website visitors when they interact with your deployed cookie consent banners and preference centers. The following cookies, referred to as `privaci cookies` by Securiti, are classified as **first-party** cookies:

| Cookie | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `__privaci_cookie_consent_uuid` | Contains a combination of unique UUID (one or more) to identify the data subject against whom you collect consent, and the current consent policy version. |
| `__privaci_cookie_consent_generated` | Contains a UUID generated by Securiti’s cookie consent SDK and latest consent policy version. |
| `__privaci_cookie_consents` | Contains the consents collected from your data subjects. |
| `__privaci_cookie_no_action` | Securiti drops this cookie when we detect a **No Action** consent type, and we don't drop this cookie again until it expires. This cookie expires after one day. |
| `__privaci_latest_published_version` | This cookie contains data from the latest published version. When you deploy and show the cookie banner with the latest published version of the cookie configuration again, we compare the current published version of your cookie configuration with the data present in this cookie. |

> [!WARNING]
> **Important**
> 
> We identify data subjects, website visitors, and end user based on a unique identifier (UUID) stored in Securiti's `privaci cookies`.
> 
> Securiti does not consider returning website visitors as new users unless they clear these `privaci cookies` from their web browser. We consider them a new user only if they clear these cookies and visit the domain again. This results in a new UUID being generated, and the cookie banner appears on your domain again. Maintaining these `privaci cookies` ensures a seamless experience for returning website visitors without repeatedly showing them the cookie banner.

## Procedure

You can also import and export cookie categorizations. For more information, see [Importing and exporting cookie categorizations](/v1/docs/importing-and-exporting-cookie-categorizations).

To categorize the cookies:

1. Once the website scan you started in [Scanning your website for cookies](/v1/docs/scanning-your-website-for-cookies) completes, choose **Cookie Consent > Domains**.
2. On the **Cookie Consent Domains** page, click the domain for which you want to categorize cookies.
3. Click the **Categorize Cookies** tab. Securiti lands you on the **Cookie & Category Details** sub-tab by default.
4. Near the upper right corner of the page, click **Edit**.
5. In the **Cookie Categories** panel along the left side of the page, click a cookie category. For instance, click the **Essential** cookie category.
6. For each cookie in the category:
  1. Evaluate the cookie name and cookie provider to determine its category.
  2. If necessary, click **Edit** (pencil icon) next to the cookie name and use the cookie category menu to reclassify the cookie.

![Reclassify-cookie-1.png](https://cdn.us.document360.io/37f15d15-eeac-4caf-9724-25918cca6992/Images/Documentation/uuid-23c9c61a-e266-6042-9c9d-b888661ca404.png)

![Reclassify-cookie-2.png](https://cdn.us.document360.io/37f15d15-eeac-4caf-9724-25918cca6992/Images/Documentation/uuid-a1d01ad8-411f-f2b5-028a-a85bd67e0376.png)
  3. In the **Purpose** box, enter a description of the cookie’s purpose.

![Purpose-box-text.png](https://cdn.us.document360.io/37f15d15-eeac-4caf-9724-25918cca6992/Images/Documentation/uuid-523e5996-747c-cb09-1a6d-648f955ad4cd.png)
7. (Optional) For each cookie category in the **Cookie Categories** panel, edit the cookie category **name** and its **description**, as shown below. The edited name and description are what data subjects and website visitors see on the **Cookie Preference Center** available from your cookie banner.

![Name-description-texts.png](https://cdn.us.document360.io/37f15d15-eeac-4caf-9724-25918cca6992/Images/Documentation/uuid-d7097c89-00c8-79c6-b902-07b0ae3f1c58.png)
8. (Opt-Out enabled cookie categories in the Cookie Categories panel) Edit the **Opt Out Text** that tells data subjects and website visitors how their experience will be affected if they opt out of the cookie category, as shown below.

![Opt-out-text.png](https://cdn.us.document360.io/37f15d15-eeac-4caf-9724-25918cca6992/Images/Documentation/uuid-731dc26a-9e72-36c9-dfde-ac8141fec6f3.png)
9. Repeat steps 5 through 8 for each of the remaining cookie categories.
10. When you are done categorizing the cookies and configuring category settings, near the upper right corner of the page, click **Save**.
11. From the upper right corner of the page, click **Deploy**.
12. On the **Publish Code** popup, from the **Change Note** section, enter the summary of the cookie configuration changes you performed, and then from the bottom of the popup, click **Publish**.

Your changes are now published and deployed to your domain. Securiti shows you a confirmation message.
13. Proceed to [Customizing your cookie banner and preference center](/v1/docs/customizing-your-cookie-banner-and-preference-center).

## Creating a custom cookie category

Securiti lets you create custom cookie categories to suit your organization's unique requirements. Custom cookie categories help you classify cookies beyond the default out-of-the-box (OOTB) options and give you flexibility in defining how each cookie category is described and consented to. You can define a name and add a description for each custom category you create.

To create a new custom cookie category:

1. On the **Cookie Consent Domains** page, select the domain for which you want to create a custom cookie category.
2. On the **Cookie Configuration** page, click the **Categorize Cookies** tab. Securiti lands you on the **Cookie & Category Details** sub-tab by default.
3. Near the upper right corner of the page, click **Edit**.
4. In the Cookie Categories panel on the left side, click **+**, and select **Add New**, as shown below.

![Custom-Cookie-Category-1.png](https://cdn.us.document360.io/37f15d15-eeac-4caf-9724-25918cca6992/Images/Documentation/uuid-05672c5a-ff56-7f96-bd4f-69cef4e1af98.png)
5. On the **Add New Cookie Category** popup:
  1. Enter a name for the custom cookie category.
  2. Add a description to explain the purpose or function of cookies in this category.

![Custom-Cookie-Category-2.png](https://cdn.us.document360.io/37f15d15-eeac-4caf-9724-25918cca6992/Images/Documentation/uuid-0489e5dc-add2-add7-e4aa-fa5605c5ae37.png)
6. Near the bottom of the popup, click **Save**.

Securiti creates the new custom cookie category with zero cookies assigned to it. You can now add cookies to this category manually or reassign cookies from existing categories.

## Reordering cookie categories

Securiti lets you reorder cookie categories to suit. Reordering categories helps you control how they appear in the cookie preference center and ensures that data subjects see them in a sequence that best fits your compliance or user experience needs. You can adjust the order of cookie categories and save the configuration to apply it across your domain.

To create a new custom order for the cookie categories:

1. In Securiti, open **Cookie Consent Management**, choose **Cookie Consent > Domains**.
2. On the **Cookie Consent Domains** page, click the domain for which you want to reorder the cookie categories.
3. Click the **Categorize Cookies** tab. Securiti lands you on the **Cookie > Category Details** subtab by default.
4. Near the upper right corner of the page, click **Edit**.
5. In the **Cookie Categories** panel along the left side of the page, mark the **Reorder Categories** checkbox, as shown below.

![](https://cdn.us.document360.io/37f15d15-eeac-4caf-9724-25918cca6992/Images/Documentation/Reorder-Cookie-Categories-Checkbox.png)
6. Use the drag handle icon next to the cookie category to move it to a new position in the list, as shown below for the **Biometrics** cookie category.
7. Once you are done with reordering the cookie categories, near the upper right corner of the page, click **Save**.

## Adding custom translations to cookie categories

You can add custom translations for the **name**, **description**, and **opt out text** of the cookie categories belonging to your domain. These custom translations also apply to the import and export cookie categorization functionality as well. For instance, you can import and export custom translations for the cookie categories in the `JSON` format.

> [!WARNING]
> **Important**
> 
> Securiti offers translation support to help localize your cookie consent experience across global audiences. However, certain terms such as **legal references**, **privacy frameworks**, and **organization** or **product names** will remain untranslated to preserve legal accuracy and brand integrity. These include, but are not limited to:
> 
> `Auti`, `AZURE`, `CCPA`, `Cookie`, `Cookies`, `CPRA`, `DIFC`, `DPL`, `DPIA`, `ePrivacy`, `GDPR`, `KYS`, `LGPD`, `PIA`, `PIPEDA`, `POPIA`, `Privacy Act`, `SCHREMS II`, and `VCDPA`.
> 
> These terms appear in their original form across all supported languages.

To add custom translations for cookie categories:

1. In Securiti , open **Cookie Consent Management**, choose **Cookie Consent > Domains**, and select a specific domain to which you want to add custom translations for its cookie categories.
2. On the **Cookie Configuration** page, click the **Categorize Cookies** tab. Securiti lands you on the **Cookie & Category Details** sub-tab by default.
3. Near the upper right corner of the page, click **Edit**, and select the cookie category that you want to customize with translations.
4. Near the upper left corner of the editable section, enable the **Provide Translation Mapping** toggle, as shown below.

![Translation-mapping.png](https://cdn.us.document360.io/37f15d15-eeac-4caf-9724-25918cca6992/Images/Documentation/uuid-92a8ac67-6db0-a43f-bdaf-ae81a7474f78.png)

> [!NOTE]
> **Note**

## Cookie category headers

The following table defines the descriptions of the cookie headers found in any cookie category belonging to a specific scanned domain, as shown below.

| Header | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Tracker Name | The name of the scanned cookie belonging to a particular cookie category. |
| Tracker Domain | The domain (URL) on which the cookie scanner found the cookie. |
| Tracker Type | It can be one of the following types of tracker: - Beacon - Cookie - Local storage item - Session storage item |
| First/Third Party | The classification type of the scanned cookie. For instance, it can either be a first-party or a third-party cookie. - First-party cookies are dropped by the scanned domain (host domain) and the subdomains belonging to the scanned base domain. - Third-party cookies are dropped by domains other than your scanned domain such as domains other than the ones your data subjects see in the address bar of their web browsers. > [!NOTE] > **Note** > > You can change this classification type using the edit mode, as shown below. > > ![First-Third-Party-Menu.png](https://cdn.us.document360.io/37f15d15-eeac-4caf-9724-25918cca6992/Images/Documentation/uuid-84fab68f-ea06-e618-a814-64a2bcab31a2.png) |
| Purpose | The purpose of the scanned cookie explaining its purpose and why it was dropped by the organization. |

![Filter-headers.png](https://cdn.us.document360.io/37f15d15-eeac-4caf-9724-25918cca6992/Images/Documentation/uuid-e3932821-16c0-5fba-0cbd-4356e0adc23b.png)

## Filtering cookie categories

You can also filter out information from the cookie categories based upon the following filter. From the **Cookie Categories** sidebar on the left side, select a cookie category and open the filter panel to apply filters on the selected cookie category, as shown below.

| Filter | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Tracker Name | Use this filter to find the scanned cookies by their names from the selected cookie category. |
| Tracker Domain | Use this filter to find the domain (URL) from the selected cookie category on which the scanned cookie was found by the scanner. |
| Service Provider | Use this filter to find cookies from specific service providers. Select the name of the cookie service provider(s) from the menu. |
| Tracker Type | Use this filter to find out cookies by the following types: - Beacon - Cookie - Local storage item - Session storage item |
| Cookie Stack Domain | Use this filter to find out cookies from the selected cookie category based on the stack domain where the cookie was found. Select one or more stack domains from the menu to filter out cookies. |
| Cookie Classification Type | Filter out cookies based on their classification types such as first-party or third-party classification type from the selected cookie category. |
