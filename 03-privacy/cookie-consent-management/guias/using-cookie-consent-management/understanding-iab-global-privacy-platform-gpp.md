# Understanding IAB Global Privacy Protocol (GPP)

IAB Global Privacy Protocol (GPP) is a global technical standard to help the digital advertising industry meet the requirements of consent preference signal detection and compliance for different jurisdictions. The GPP enables data subject consent signals to be communicated throughout the digital supply chain. It also provides the protocol to help consolidate the management of different consent signals from multiple global privacy jurisdictions by streamlining technical privacy standards into a singular schema and set of tools that can adapt to regulatory and commercial market demands across channels. The GPP moves beyond GDPR compliance to set up the industry to more easily adapt to data protection regulations as they emerge around the world.

> [!NOTE]
> **Note**
> 
> Securiti currently supports **IAB EU TCF v2.3** and GPC consent signals within the GPP.

| Consent Signals | Description |
| --- | --- |
| IAB Europe TCF | It is the original consent string or signal for the IAB EU **Publishers** and **Vendors** to comply with consent requirements of the EU GDPR and e-privacy directive. The **IAB EU TCF v2.3** compliance framework lets you collect the consent of your data subjects for all purposes and vendors. It also lets the data subjects to withdraw their consent as well as maintain records of consent. Securiti lets you to record consent status of data subjects and pass on the consent string to the downstream IAB vendors and ultimately ensures compliance with the **IAB EU TCF v2.3**. |
| Global Privacy Control (GPC) | It is a data subject controlled browser plugin which is recognized as a **global opt-out preference signal**. When the GPC is detected on the browser/device of data subjects, they are automatically opted out of third party cookies involved in the sale and sharing of personal information such as selected cookie categories. Securiti lets you to comply with the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA) by detecting and honoring the GPC signal on the browser/device of data subjects and by opting the data subjects out of third party cookies involved in the sale and sharing of personal information. It also lets you alert your data subjects whether the GPC has been detected on their browser/devices via the GPC flyout feature to achieve ultimate compliance with the CPRA. |

The Global Privacy Platform (GPP) is a protocol designed by the Interactive Advertising Bureau (IAB) Tech Lab to streamline and standardize the transmission of consumer consent choice signals from websites and applications to IAB enrolled providers. The framework enables user consent signals to be communicated throughout the digital ad supply chain

The Interactive Advertising Bureau (IAB) is a trade association representing advertising businesses. It develops industry standards, conducts research, and provides legal support for the online advertising industry. Cookie Consent Management module lets you configure your cookie banners and preference centers with two IAB privacy consent frameworks: the **EU TCF v2.2** and the **MSPA U.S. National**.

The Transparency and Consent Framework (TCF) is a voluntary consent standardization protocol tool developed by the IAB Europe. It facilitates publishers, vendors, and consent management platforms (CMPs) to comply with certain provisions of the ePrivacy Directive and the GDPR.

The Global Privacy Control (GPC) is a browser-based global opt-out tool that lets the Internet users signal their opt-out consent settings to websites, advertisers, and publishers on the world wide web. Some global privacy laws, such as the CPRA, require covered entities to mandatorily detect and honor users’ GPC signal. The Cookie Consent Management module lets you configure cookie banners and preference centers with GPC to ensure detection of the GPC signals on the web browsers of your data subjects to honor their opt-out cookie preferences

Signal means any signal defined by the IAB EU TCF v2.2 **Policies** or **Specifications** sent by a CMP to **Vendors**, usually on behalf of a **Publisher**. This includes information about the transparency, consent, and/or objection status of a Vendor and/or Purpose, the opt-in status of a Special Feature, and Publisher restrictions.

The California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA) amended the CCPA by adding additional consumer privacy rights and obligations for businesses. It also created the California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA), tasked with enforcing the law and educating the public on their rights and obligations under the law.
